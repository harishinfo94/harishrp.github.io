Where reliability meets AI

Each project starts from a maintenance question, uses AI for the part that is too much to read or watch by hand, and leaves the decision with people.

Wireless vibration monitoring

Live
  1. The question

    Is this machine starting to fail?

  2. What the AI does

    Machine learning watches vibration from 250+ assets around the clock and flags developing faults early.

  3. Who decides

    Reliability engineers confirm the finding and plan the repair.

Maintenance troubleshooting agent

In build
  1. The question

    What does this alarm mean, and what fixed it last time?

  2. What the AI does

    A language-model agent searches manuals, alarm codes, spare parts and work history, then answers with its sources.

  3. Who decides

    The technician at the machine.

AI on maintenance history

In pilot
  1. The question

    Do our maintenance plans match how equipment really fails?

  2. What the AI does

    Language models read years of work orders to find failure modes, missing spare parts and records that do not add up.

  3. Who decides

    Planners, reliability engineers and supervisors approve every change.

Equipment health models

In build
  1. The question

    Which assets in a fleet are drifting from normal?

  2. What the AI does

    Models score each asset from process and motor-current data and rank the fleet every week.

  3. Who decides

    Engineers choose which assets to inspect first.

Capital replacement planning

Planned
  1. The question

    Should we run, refurbish or replace this asset, and when?

  2. What the AI does

    Failure-probability models combine asset health, criticality and cost into a recommendation.

  3. Who decides

    Engineering, finance and site leadership.

The AI does the reading. People make the call.

Master’s thesis, University of Illinois Chicago, 2021

Process Analysis of Breakage and Cracks in Pharmaceutical Vials Using DMAIC Problem Solving Methodology

Glass vials that crack on a fill and finish line threaten sterility and stop production. I used the DMAIC method to find where and why vials break, starting with a statistical analysis of rejection rates from the empty-vial inspection machine across four vial sizes, from 50 ml to 500 ml.

Read the thesis at UIC DOI 10.25417/uic.15273951
  1. DDefine the breakage problem and its cost in downtime
  2. MMeasure rejection rates by vial size
  3. AAnalyse with SPC, hypothesis tests and ANOVA
  4. IImprove the operations that cause breakage
  5. CControl so the gains hold
